FlyRank ML Internship · Capstone Research

A Machine Learning-Based Content Opportunity Scoring System for Search Refresh Prioritization

Using historical search visibility and engagement signals to prioritize content pages for review and refresh.

Author: Abubakar Shahid

Abstract Problem Data Methodology Results Limitations Recommendations Reproducibility Acknowledgments

Abstract

This research asks whether historical search and engagement signals can be used to identify content pages that should be prioritized for refresh review.

The analysis uses the FlyRank Search Intelligence warehouse, containing daily content performance observations across a large collection of anonymized pages.

Search visibility and engagement features are transformed into an opportunity-scoring workflow and evaluated with a machine-learning model alongside a simple baseline.

The resulting system produces a ranked list of content opportunities together with signal-based reasons that can support editorial prioritization.

The result should be interpreted as a decision-support and ranking system based on observed historical signals, rather than evidence that refreshing a page will causally improve Google rankings, clicks, or traffic.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams often have many pages that could potentially be improved, but limited time to review every page. The practical decision is therefore not simply whether a page performs well or poorly, but which pages deserve attention first.

This capstone investigates whether historical search visibility and engagement signals can be combined into a repeatable content opportunity scoring workflow.

Research Question: Can historical search and engagement signals be used to identify content pages that should be refreshed first?

The intended outcome is a ranked action list that helps a content team decide where to spend review and refresh effort.

2. Data

The analysis is built on the FlyRank ML Internship Search Intelligence warehouse release. The warehouse provides historical content, search, and engagement information suitable for studying search-performance signals.

78.8M+ Daily records
427K+ Unique pages
2025–2026 Historical window
Daily Performance grain

Data Tables

The warehouse includes dimensional and fact-style tables used to construct the analysis. The capstone works primarily with content and daily performance information.

  • dim_clients — client-level metadata.
  • dim_content — content/page metadata.
  • fact_daily — daily performance observations.
  • fact_daily_sample — sampled daily data.
  • fact_query_90d — query-level historical information.

Signals Used

Signal group Examples Purpose
Search visibility Impressions, average position Measure search exposure
Search response Clicks Measure search-result capture
Traffic Sessions, pageviews Measure resulting site activity
Engagement Engaged sessions, scroll events Measure interaction quality
Public-safety rule:

Client names, domains, private queries, credentials, raw exports, and other private identifiers are intentionally excluded from this public paper.

3. Methodology

The analysis follows a repeatable pipeline that converts historical search and engagement observations into an opportunity ranking.

01 · Data Preparation Historical content and performance records are filtered and aggregated for analysis.
02 · Feature Engineering Search visibility, clicks, traffic, and engagement signals are transformed into modeling features.
03 · Opportunity Score Signals are combined into a score representing relative refresh-review opportunity.
04 · Baseline A simple baseline is used as a reference point for model evaluation.
05 · Random Forest A Random Forest model is trained to model the opportunity scoring relationship.
06 · Ranking Pages are ranked by opportunity score and converted into actionable recommendations.

Feature Design

The feature set is based on signals available in the historical warehouse. The central idea is to combine search visibility with user-response and engagement information rather than relying on a single metric.

Target / Label

The modeling target represents the opportunity score constructed within the capstone workflow. Therefore, the machine-learning experiment should be interpreted primarily as a ranking/scoring exercise.

Model

A Random Forest regression model is used to learn the relationship between the engineered signals and the opportunity score.

Validation

The model is evaluated against a baseline on the same evaluation data. The capstone also considers the important distinction between reproducing a constructed score and genuinely predicting future content performance.

Leakage / target-construction caution

Because the target is constructed from signals related to the features used by the model, a very strong model fit should not be interpreted as evidence that the system can predict future SEO outcomes. The strongest interpretation is that the model can reproduce or operationalize the defined opportunity-scoring rule.

4. Results

The completed capstone compares the Random Forest model against a baseline and uses feature importance to understand which signals contribute to the resulting ranking.

Model Performance

Approach MAE Interpretation
Baseline 41.37 Reference error level
Random Forest 0.223 Much lower error when reproducing the constructed score

R²

The completed notebook reports an R² of approximately 0.997 for the Random Forest experiment.

How to interpret this result

The extremely high R² should not be presented as proof of predictive power over future Google performance. The target is derived from the same family of historical signals available to the model. The result therefore indicates that the model is highly effective at reproducing the constructed opportunity score under the experiment, rather than demonstrating causal or future-ranking prediction.

What the Results Support

  • A repeatable scoring workflow can convert multiple performance signals into a prioritized content list.
  • The Random Forest can closely reproduce the defined opportunity score.
  • Feature importance can help explain which available signals are most influential within the model.
  • The resulting ranking can be used as a starting point for editorial review.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

This work is designed as search-intelligence decision support, not as a causal experiment.

Observed

The analysis observes historical relationships between search visibility, clicks, traffic, engagement, and the constructed opportunity score.

Directional

The resulting rankings provide directional evidence about which pages may deserve closer editorial review.

Decision Support

The system should be used to prioritize human review, not to automatically decide that a page must be rewritten, merged, pruned, or refreshed.

What this study does NOT prove

  • It does not prove Google's ranking algorithm.
  • It does not prove that refreshing a page causes ranking improvement.
  • It does not establish causal impact on clicks or traffic.
  • It does not guarantee future performance for a ranked page.

6. Ranked Recommendations

The scoring engine is intended to produce a prioritized action queue. The exact pages are represented using safe/anonymized identifiers in the public version.

Priority 1

Review high-opportunity pages first

Start with pages receiving the highest opportunity scores. These pages should receive human review before any refresh decision is made.

Recommended action: Refresh review.
Priority 2

Investigate visibility without proportional response

Pages with meaningful search exposure but weaker response signals can be reviewed for title, metadata, search-intent alignment, and content presentation.

Recommended action: Metadata and content review.
Priority 3

Review engagement opportunities

Pages showing weaker engagement signals can be reviewed for content usefulness, structure, readability, and alignment with visitor intent.

Recommended action: Content and UX review.
Priority 4

Monitor borderline opportunities

Medium-ranked pages should be monitored rather than immediately rewritten. Additional historical evidence can determine whether they move into a higher-priority group.

Recommended action: Monitor.

Conclusion

This capstone demonstrates a practical search-intelligence workflow for turning historical search and engagement signals into a ranked content-review queue.

The Random Forest model closely reproduces the constructed opportunity score, while the ranking layer converts the analysis into actionable content-review priorities.

The most appropriate use of the system is therefore as a decision-support tool: it helps teams decide what to investigate first while leaving the final refresh decision to human editorial judgment.
